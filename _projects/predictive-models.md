### The Challenge
Predicting mental health outcomes after natural disasters is valuable for directing support resources, but the underlying data tends to be noisy, incomplete, and high-dimensional - making it easy to overfit or draw misleading conclusions without careful preprocessing and model selection.

### Approach
- Engineered predictive models using **Linear Regression** and **Decision Tree Regressors**, tuning hyperparameters to improve performance.
- Preprocessed the dataset with **imputation, encoding techniques, and dimensionality reduction** to optimize feature selection and reduce noise.
- Used **Scikit-learn** for model training and evaluation, and **Matplotlib** for data visualization to support both data cleaning decisions and interpretation of model results.

### Results
- Produced two working predictive models (Linear Regression and Decision Tree) for forecasting post-disaster mental health outcomes.
- Improved model performance through systematic hyperparameter tuning and dimensionality reduction.

### Key Achievements
-  Built and compared two predictive modeling approaches for a socially impactful forecasting problem
-  Applied dimensionality reduction and imputation to handle noisy, high-dimensional data
-  Used visualization to support both preprocessing decisions and model interpretability
